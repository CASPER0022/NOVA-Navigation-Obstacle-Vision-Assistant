A. Implement the "Clock-Position" System
The paper mentions that visually impaired users prefer direction guidance using the clock face (e.g., "Person at 11 o'clock", "Chair at 1 o'clock") over simple "Left/Right" descriptions.

How it works: Divide the horizontal screen space (0% to 100%) into clock numbers from 9 o'clock (far left) to 3 o'clock (far right), where 12 o'clock is directly straight ahead.
B. Prioritize by Proximity (Distance)
Right now, the code warns about whatever object YOLO processes first (obstacles_detected[0]). If a bottle is far away but a person is right in front of you, it might warn about the bottle first!

How it works: Sort the detected obstacles by how close they are (proximity or width_ratio) and announce the closest obstacle first.
C. Draw Direction Arrows on the Video Window
For presentation/grading purposes, drawing visual indicators (like red arrows pointing to the nearest hazard) makes the GUI look premium and highly interactive.
